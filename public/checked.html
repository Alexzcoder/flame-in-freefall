Flame in Freefall

How we checked our numbers

Every claim on this site comes with how it was tested. Labels: CROSS-VALIDATEDscored on whole NASA test days the model never saw · HELD OUTone set of test days kept aside · FITreproduces cases it was tuned on (not a prediction).

1. The data

1,624 NASA microgravity combustion runs from 24 investigations in NASA's Physical Sciences Informatics archive, one schema, every value traced to its source file and row. 5,123 numbers re-read from NASA's raw files and matched. Assumed values (e.g. cabin pressure when not recorded) and censored burn times are flagged on every run.

3. The flame simulation HELD OUT

A 2D fluid + combustion simulation, tuned on NASA gas-flame outcomes (331 runs), BASS-II flame shapes measured from 384 NASA videos, and solid burn-or-out cases. Scored on test days the tuning never saw.

What it did not: gas-flame outcomes are no better than guessing on test days, so gas-flame and droplet numbers on this site come from the data models above. Flame shapes did not hold up on held-out runs, so the flames you see in the simulator are illustrative.

4. Flame spread FIT LEAVE-ONE-OUT

Seven NASA targets (BASS-II Summary Report NASA/TM-20210011385; SAFFIRE-II): spread speeds and the airflow below which a flame goes out. Fit: 5 of 7 matched. Predicted with each target left out: pending.

5. The instant predictor (neural network)

A small neural network trained on 20,000 runs of the tuned simulation, so the site can answer instantly. Tested on 2,000 held-out simulations: it predicts whether a flame lights in 99.4% and whether it goes out in 97.9% of cases. It copies the simulation, errors included.

6. What we do not claim

  • Every NASA run here was in microgravity. Moon and Earth gravity effects come from physics, not data.
  • Spread between objects (safe separation distances) uses standard fire-engineering heat-radiation estimates with typical laboratory values, not NASA microgravity data, and ignores fire travelling along cables or bags.
  • Above 22 % oxygen no solid material has been burned in microgravity in NASA's public data: anything there is an extrapolation.
