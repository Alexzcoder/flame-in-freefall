Flame lab

A live fluid simulation of a small fire. Change gravity, oxygen and airflow and watch the flame's shape change.

What the simulation does
  • Air is a 2D fluid (stable-fluids solver). Fuel vapour, oxygen, heat and smoke are carried by it.
  • The flame burns only where fuel vapour meets oxygen and it is hot enough. Burning releases heat and soot; the hot surface releases more fuel vapour.
  • Hot gas rises in proportion to gravity. With no gravity nothing rises, so oxygen only arrives by diffusion or the fan.
  • The flame loses heat by radiation, which matters most when the flow is slow (the quenching NASA's BASS-II measured).
  • Illustrative: the shapes and trends are physical, the numbers are not calibrated to NASA tests. Compare with the real footage →
